### @GaryMarcus · 2026-10-30T14:19:59.721000Z

> In this new paper, we make a strong point:
> 
> Human imitation is a misplaced goal for AI.
> 
> Too much of AI development is based on human imitation, as exemplified by the Turing test: the more a machine resembles us, the more successful it is.
> 
> But there are two major problems.
> 
> The first is conceptual. Imitating human outputs does not tell us much about the capacities behind them:
> 
> The same answer can emerge from different processes. A plausible response does not tell us whether a system can reason reliably, generalize to unfamiliar situations, or recognize when it should say “I don’t know”. 
> 
> The second is ethical. Why should we want AI that imitates humans?
> 
> Humans are biased. Our attention is limited. Our judgment is fallible. Reproducing these limitations is hardly an ambitious vision for AI.
> 
> Even worse:
> 
> A system that impersonates a person can exploit human trust.
> 
> And an industry focused on replicating humans can fall in the “Turing’s trap”: favour human replacement over augmentation. 
> 
> Instead, we argue that AI should aim to improve human welfare.
> 
> To this end, we propose three coordinated changes:
> 
> For developers: Build systems that strengthen human capabilities and independent judgment.
> 
> For funders: Require projects to assess benefits, costs, and who receives or bears them.
> 
> For institutions: Base procurement and renewal on demonstrated benefits.
> 
> The goal should be to help people know more, suffer less, and exercise greater control over their lives.
>

> 
> Full paper in the first reply.
>
